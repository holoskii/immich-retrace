O · all years

Overview timeline

The problem every variant has to solve: 18 years is about 6 600 days, so on a single linear axis a 10-day trip is about 1.5 px wide and too narrow to grab. All three share one selection. Drag to select, drag the edges to resize, drag the middle to move, or pick an album.

AO-A

Context and focus

A thin strip shows all years with a viewport window. Below it is a zoomed wave with album bars, where you make the selection. Scroll to zoom, or drag the window.

  • Standard, predictable pattern
  • Album rail makes trips clickable
  • Two strips, two things to drag
scroll = zoom · drag = select · edges = resize · bars = albums (click)
BO-B

Ridgeline by year

One row per year with January to December across, so seasons line up. A 10-day trip is about 30 px wide with no zooming.

  • Whole life on one screen, trips readable
  • Shows yearly habits (September trips)
  • A selection can't cross New Year
  • 2008–2014 (20 items) is folded away
drag inside a row = select · edges = resize
CO-C

Fisheye stream

All years always fit the width, and the selection is magnified in place with compressed shoulders around it. Photos are drawn above the axis and videos mirrored below.

  • One strip, no zoom state
  • Video share visible at a glance
  • Non-linear axis; distances outside the lens lie
  • The lens re-settles after each drag
drag edges/middle = adjust lens · drag outside = new selection
D · selected range

Photo-wave

Your option (b): the density curve is built from the previews themselves. Shown here: 5–15 Sep 2025, all 502 items in that range, including photos outside the album. Hovering moves the shared time cursor, and the maps below follow it.

AD-A

Stacked columns

Two-hour bins with the tiles stacked from the baseline. The silhouette is the histogram, read exactly.

  • Honest counts, and tiles align in a tidy grid
  • Easy to make zoomable (bin = f(zoom))
  • Bins hide the exact time of a shot
BD-B

Swarm on a wave

Every tile sits at its exact capture time and is nudged up only when it would overlap. A soft density curve shows behind.

  • Most organic “wave” look
  • Bursts read as spikes, quiet hours as gaps
  • Stack height isn't a count you can read off
CD-C

Day rows

One row per day, 00:00 to 24:00 across, tiles stacked per half hour. It reads like a contact sheet of a trip.

  • Daily rhythm is obvious (early starts, long drives)
  • Labels per day: weekday, count, city
  • Gets tall for long ranges; needs a different mode above ~3 weeks
M · where

Map

Your option (c): previews pinned on the map. 295 of the 502 items have GPS; the other 207 (mostly camera shots) don't appear here. Gaps of more than 4 h between points are dashed instead of drawn as a straight walk. The scrubber under M-B moves the same cursor as hovering the photo-wave.

AM-A

Route with pins

The route is colored by time, dim at the start and bright at the end, with clustered photo pins and a count on each.

  • Direction of travel without animation
  • Pins show what each place looked like
  • Line and pins compete in dense cities
BM-B

Progress trail

The whole route is drawn grey. The part travelled so far is bright up to the cursor, with a marker and the nearest photo next to it.

  • Best for the “hover and watch where I was” moment
  • Quiet when you're not hovering
  • Shows little until you hover
CM-C

Photo piles

No line, only stacks of previews where you shot, sized by count, with city names on the biggest piles. The route fades in up to the cursor while you hover.

  • Most visual; the map becomes a mosaic
  • No misleading straight lines at all
  • Order of places is lost without hover
M-D · your combo + the zoom question

Map that follows time

M-A's route and pins, with M-B's strip, scrubber and photo pop-out underneath. The open question is zoom. If the whole trip is fitted, Osh and Gul'cha shrink to a few pixels and the marker crawls. In Follow mode the camera fits the GPS points within ±3 h of the cursor instead. It moves only when the marker leaves the middle 70% of the frame or the needed zoom changes by more than 1.8×. That way it holds still inside a city and pulls back for a driving day. The inset keeps the whole trip in view.

DM-D

Follow-cam

Run the mouse along the strip under the map. Smooth is a camera path computed ahead of time from time-weighted neighbours (σ 1.5 h, blended out to 6 h / 24 h / whole trip where GPS is sparse), followed by a critically damped spring; far jumps pull back first. Reactive is the first version. In the inset, the dashed frame is where the camera is heading.

  • City walks get street-level scale, drives get the whole valley
  • No step changes: the target itself is continuous in time
  • Lags the cursor by ~0.5 s by design
G · one photo

Gallery / lightbox

Opens on click and goes through the selected range in time order. Here the real photo is replaced by its blurred thumbhash. Click the left or right half, or hover the frame and use ← →.

AG-A

Cinema

Black, full-bleed photo with a filmstrip of neighbours. Metadata stays out of the way at the top.

  • Photo gets every pixel
  • Filmstrip shows bursts and what's next
  • No sense of where or when beyond one line
BG-B

Photo and logbook

The photo takes about 70% of the width. The side panel carries the time, place, coordinates, a mini-map and the position in the range.

  • Keeps the time/place story while browsing
  • “Open in Immich” and “original” live here
  • Smaller photo
CG-C

Scrubber

Full-bleed photo with a thin density strip of the whole range along the bottom. Drag it to jump through hours at a time.

  • Fast long jumps without closing the lightbox
  • Keeps the wave metaphor in the lightbox
  • Strip covers the bottom of the photo
